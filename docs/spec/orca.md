### R98 — Rebind an Orca runtime change only on proven continuity

Type: H · Scenarios: [A195](#a195), [A196](#a196), [A197](#a197), [A198](#a198), [A199](#a199)

For the recorded owner only (non-owner detection in R105 takes precedence regardless of the supplied owner string), when an objective's recorded runtime differs from the current one, each mutating runtime check (`require_authority`, the Governor projection, report reads, admission recovery and new worker admission) first classifies continuity from run-current and the exact worker and Run/Task reads Pod already makes, each at the current runtime. Equal Run, coordinator handle, recorded consumer generation and bound admission Run/Task/Dispatch/worker/worktree ids prove it: Pod rebinds under the objective lock, records old and new runtime, time, verified identities and provenance `automatic`, reports the rebind and continues. An unreadable identity, an unresolved admission, a reserved admission whose exact Run/Task lookup is unavailable, inconclusive or finds a Dispatch, a referenced Run run-current cannot show, or no recorded generation leaves it ambiguous: Pod writes nothing and refuses `runtime_continuity_ambiguous`, naming what it could not read. Only a direct `user_direct` Owner decision through `internal runtime-continuity`, naming the objective, both runtimes and that ambiguity and reclassified at write time, rebinds it with provenance `owner`; it changes only the recorded runtime and a missing generation, with R120's decision-channel marker accompanying the owner decision. Any differing identity disproves continuity: the path keeps its existing refusal and nothing rebinds it. A bind, a refusal deferral, the report path's route updates and report consumption apply native evidence only at the runtime they read it at: none rebinds inside its own authority join, which refuses with its existing code as without continuity, and each refuses `native_authority_unverified` without a write when another call rebound first. Recording a start receipt's request fields may rebind; the bind that follows still refuses a receipt from another runtime. Status shows the bounded continuity history, including handoffs, when there is one, and never writes; a rebind past eight entries refuses without a write. A rebind never changes the coordinator or owner, creates or adopts a Run, or starts, replays or recovers work; R25/R45 recovery stays a separate step.

### R105 — Explicit Owner-authorized coordinator loss handoff

Type: H · Scenarios: [A234](#a234), [A235](#a235), [A236](#a236), [A237](#a237), [A238](#a238), [A239](#a239), [A240](#a240), [A241](#a241), [A242](#a242), [A243](#a243), [A244](#a244), [A245](#a245), [A246](#a246), [A247](#a247), [A248](#a248), [A249](#a249), [A264](#a264)

Only `internal owner-handoff` may replace an objective coordinator. Read-only detection in status and authority refusals precedes R98 for a non-owner caller regardless of its supplied owner string. The previous coordinator is always the recorded Pod owner. A live non-worker caller must resolve a stable identical recorded Run, whose coordinator is either that owner (fresh), or this caller with a matching durable pending handoff (resume). Orca must report the recorded owner's handle stale or gone; structured-session unsupported and unreadable states fail closed. Every bound admission's Run/Task/Dispatch/worker/worktree identity is compared at the current runtime by R98. The confirmation alone may cover named R98 ambiguities, never differing identities or a different Run. Another open objective on the Run blocks handoff and is named. Scope enumeration must be complete and readable, including objective directories; a referenced peer is excluded only by a fully validated, noncontradictory obligation closure and its matching report. Unavailable scope or unsupported closure cannot prove nonmembership. One exception: a record on an older version of a current Pod schema family proves it is not on the Run when its documented Run references (checkpoint native_refs runId, admission run_id) are all readable and the Run id appears in none of its values; a future or unknown schema still holds. A peer continued by a successor (R110) that references the Run still blocks, and the objective itself, once continued, refuses handoff as `objective_continued`. The direct Owner decision to hold handoff also bars exclusion of a closed peer when retained REUSE changed definition and its original eligibility scope is unrecorded. Recorded facts remain readable for ordinary writes; only handoff holds with a bounded named unverifiable-peer diagnosis. No confirmation overrides it, and a pending handoff remains pending. Report/packet scope, editing boundary and the later definition never replace the missing original scope.

The plain question names the objective and owner change, says Orca reports the previous coordinator's handle stale or gone, and contains no handle or Run id. The coordinator builds a `user_direct` decision with instruction and exactly objective, from_owner, to_owner, run, recorded_generation, recorded_runtime, current_runtime and ambiguity from fresh status facts. A missing recorded generation is a named ambiguity; the decision binds the recorded generation, allowing a resume after generation advances. The operation rebuilds facts under the objective lock and refuses absent, broad, stale or mismatched confirmation, closed objectives and new entries past the shared eight-entry bound before phase 1.

Phase 1 writes an owner_handoff pending history entry with its decision, both owners, runtimes, recorded Run/generation and pre-rebind liveness/lineage evidence, accompanied by R120's required decision-channel marker. Branch 1 alone calls `orchestration run-use --id RUN --json` from this caller; Branch 2 never calls it again. Phase 2 rechecks exact work identity and a stable Run naming this caller with generation above the recorded one (only coordinator identity when no generation was recorded). It writes done, objective/admission owners, the new continuity binding and current runtimes on references/admissions. The revision counter may advance; the Governor journal and other objectives stay byte-identical. No work start, replay, retry, recovery, settlement, adoption, redispatch, closure or release occurs.

A refusal/error aborts only after authoritative readback proves the Run still names the recorded owner at the recorded generation; unknown/landed results or failed rereads remain pending. A pending caller alone may resume while its handle is not reported stale or gone; a different live caller cannot abort it. The operation aborts definite eligibility loss (lost pending caller, different Run/identity or another open objective), preserving the reason; unreadable facts leave pending. A new attempt replaces an aborted entry without adding history, and retries retain pending. At most one entry is unfinished. Manual rebinds, aborted or crashed handoffs without live pending lineage require a successor or recovery objective, never a manual run-use. Status/refusals never abort or write.

Only handle, liveness/runtime and bounded native code leave terminal show; preview, title, paths, branch, pty and pane identity never enter handoff records or outputs and never grant authority. After done, ordinary native readback may settle failed reviewers; reserved and unresolved attempts retain R25/R45. Old handles refuse. Genuine 0.8.0 objective status refuses owner_handoff provenance as state_unsupported without conversion; its run-scoped reader skips the record.

### R110 — A successor's claim ends a lost coordinator's authority

Type: H · Scenarios: [A260](#a260), [A261](#a261), [A262](#a262)

A successor objective may claim its predecessor before it starts work, through `internal succession` from the successor's live recorded coordinator with the successor's Run authority. The claim ends the predecessor's write authority and gives it to no one: its owner, admissions, Runs, map and evidence stay as recorded, and nothing is converted, started, settled or released; only the Governor rows R118 carries are copied into the successor. Without a decision the operation returns fresh facts and a plain question naming both objectives and no handle or Run, and writes nothing. Only a `user_direct` decision with the owner's instruction and exactly `predecessor`, `predecessor_owner`, `successor`, `successor_owner`, `runs`, `ambiguity` and the `carried` rows (R118) from those facts permits the claim; a verified owner continuation instruction may be relayed and recorded with `channel: relayed` and its complete relay under R120. A mailbox message is never itself a decision; the recorded decision conveys the owner's verified instruction, never the coordinator's inference from a message.

It never steals. Orca must report the predecessor's recorded coordinator handle stale or gone, or show it exited: not connected or writable, with a confirmed exit cause (`operator_close`, `signaled` or `exited`), as Orca keeps a deliberately closed terminal's record; an `unknown` cause, such as a stop Orca never confirmed, is not an exit. Each predecessor Run's current coordinator, read by run show, must be absent, that handle, or another handle that is stale, gone or exited. Owner handoff (R105) still requires stale or gone; every Dispatch on its Runs and every bound admission must read settled with its exact recorded identities, from a complete worker list; and it may have no unsettled Governor effect that R118 cannot carry, unfinished owner handoff or unresolved admission. A reserved admission with no Dispatch is a named ambiguity the decision must carry, and the claim records it; one with a Dispatch holds. The successor must be recorded, open and not started: no admission, Governor work, settled obligation, delivery, closure or other continuation. The predecessor must be a readable current record of the same repository, open and unclaimed, not the successor itself, and not itself the successor of an incomplete claim (R118).

Detection never writes the predecessor or completes a link; the successor's own authority join may record an ordinary R98 rebind. Under both objective locks, taken in path order, the successor first records its reservation (R118), then the predecessor records `continued_by` (successor, record, owner, Runs, time, instruction, unfinished obligations, unreported settled admissions, accepted ambiguity and `orca_claim: unavailable`), then the successor's Governor journal receives the carried rows (R118), then the successor records `continues`. An exact replay with a decision writes nothing new and completes a successor link an interrupted claim left out; status reads that successor `claim_incomplete` until then, and a successor that already continues another objective refuses `successor_started`. A second claim refuses `predecessor_claimed`. Afterwards every write of the predecessor, including its own recorded coordinator's checkpoint, admission, report, Governor, runtime-continuity and worker-start paths, and any owner handoff, refuses `objective_continued` naming the successor; status still reads it. Status lists its open admissions as settled by the claim (`settlement: claim`, or `claim_no_dispatch` with its basis and a render line for a named reserved row), never unverified, and shows the claim's accepted ambiguity. Detection of a recorded claim returns that claim's facts, so its own decision completes an interrupted link. The R108 list keeps its phase `continued` and shows `succession_state` `successor_open` until the successor chain (at most eight) ends in a closed objective, then `successor_closed`; that means the successor closed, not that the predecessor's obligations were proven. A predecessor whose earlier successor closed without a claim is continued by a fresh successor that claims it, then closes. A cleanup plan for the predecessor protects every worktree its successors record, and all its worktrees when a successor's location is unrecorded or unreadable, or the chain loops or exceeds eight successors. A native-start evidence file for a start Orca accepted before the claim is still kept beside the record; the record itself is not written. Orca offers no worktree claim or conditional send (R111), so the claim binds none; a prompt Orca already accepted for the old terminal is outside Pod's control. The claim is a cooperative Pod record, not an operating-system fence: it does not stop a process of the old coordinator that is still running.

### R111 — Report the message route to a Run's current coordinator

Type: B · Scenarios: [A263](#a263)

A message from a sender outside any Orca terminal must reach only the terminal that still coordinates a Run. Only Orca can provide that, as a send it evaluates at delivery against the Run's coordinator, or as a sender identity for an administrator without a terminal. Orca advertises neither, so `pod doctor --json` reports `message_route` with `conditional_send` and `administrator_sender` `unavailable`, its basis and the Orca request as Pod's dependency. Pod never sends with another pane's identity. When Orca advertises such a capability, Pod reports that form after a reviewed mapping, never by inference from help text. Until then the administrator relay is the available form, by owner decision (pod#52 PoD#4 as amended on 2026-10-13): an administrator sends from its own Orca terminal, bound to no Run, to `run:<id>`, and only the coordinator bound to that Run when it checks reads the message. After the Run moves, the earlier coordinator reads nothing, whether the message was sent before or after the move, and a replay with the same retry id returns the same message; a probe on Orca 1.4.224 showed each of these. Doctor reports `administrator_relay` available when Orca advertises its orchestration contract, with its address, sender and basis apart from the other two forms. Coordinators read the Run mailbox with `orca orchestration check` at each transition and acknowledge each delivery; a coordinator learns of a message only when it checks. A mailbox message is never itself a decision. Under R120, a permitted relayed decision is the owner's verified decision carried by the mailbox and recorded as `relayed`, never the coordinator's inference from a message. The coordinator classifies the channel honestly; Pod cannot prove where a keystroke came from.

### R112 — The owner's historical retirement of delivered work whose coordinator is lost

Type: H · Scenarios: [A266](#a266), [A267](#a267), [A268](#a268), [A269](#a269), [A270](#a270), [A352](#a352), [A353](#a353), [A354](#a354)

An objective whose deliverable is proved elsewhere but whose recorded coordinator is permanently gone may be ended by the owner's historical retirement through `internal historical-retirement`, from any caller: it needs no Orca terminal, live coordinator, Run binding or successor, and it starts no agent session, worker or successor. Retirement releases only Pod's own record of the named worktrees, so that CE's cleanup can recheck and remove them; it is no Delivery, cleanup, release or lifecycle mutation of Orca, Git or a provider (A94). The request names each objective and the worktree paths it releases. Without a decision it writes nothing and returns, per objective, either fresh facts or one distinct refusal, and one plain question naming the eligible objectives and the paths to release, never a handle or Run. The question also names each refused objective with its condition: one refused after every fact was read keeps complete facts, so it is named with its path as one a decision that names it retires once the condition clears and its facts still match; any other is named as not retirable by a decision on these facts. The next action asks for a request and decision with only the objectives the owner's answer names. A refusal names an objective with no readable record (`objective_unknown`) or no recorded coordinator (`owner_unrecorded`); one closed (`objective_closed`), continued by a successor (`objective_continued`), continuing a predecessor it claimed, or named as its successor by another record's claim, even one interrupted before the back-link (`objective_continues`, since retiring a successor would leave that chain neither open nor closed) or already under historical retirement (`objective_retired`); a recorded coordinator Orca does not report stale or gone, or show exited with a confirmed cause as R110 reads it (`owner_not_lost`); a recorded Run now bound to another coordinator or a consumer generation that differs from the recorded binding (`generation_changed`), except when the lost or confirmed-exited coordinator was already proved gone and Run show explicitly reports `coordinator_handle: null`; an omitted field or the same recorded coordinator at a changed generation still refuses; an unfinished owner handoff (`handoff_pending`); an incomplete or unreadable worker list, a Run, bound Dispatch or worker Dispatch that is unreadable or differs from its recorded identity, or a worker list whose runtime or worker Dispatch identity Pod could not record or pass back to Orca exactly (not UTF-8 text without NUL, or a runtime over 256 characters), refused before Pod uses it (`workers_unverified`); any such Dispatch live or unsettled, or a start request of an unresolved or reserved admission that Orca still reads pending (`work_live`, an unreadable one `workers_unverified`); a terminal Orca lists in a path to release that is not proven exited (`path_in_use`), or a path Orca's worktree show does not resolve exactly, or whose terminal list is partial or names another worktree (`path_unverified`), each path classified under R113; a path that is not one of the objective's recorded worktrees (`path_not_recorded`); and an unreadable or unsupported record or Governor journal, an unreadable Pod record that could hold a claim naming it (a directory without a record carries none), no recorded Run or more than 64 preserved records (`scope_unreadable`). The claim, closure and earlier retirement checks precede every native read. An unknown name creates no state. An unresolved or reserved admission and a pending, UNKNOWN or FAILED Governor row do not refuse by themselves: they are listed as preserved records, and the complete worker lists of the objective's Runs prove that no Dispatch for them is live.

Only a `user_direct` decision with its R120 channel (and complete verified-source relay when relayed), the owner's instruction and exactly `repository` (the repository key), `reason` and, for each requested objective in order, `objective`, `owner` (the recorded handle), `runs` (Run and its consumer generation), `release`, R113's `locations`, `retained` and `unrecorded_locations`, `records` (the full preserved Governor and admission table with current states, each open admission marking whether Pod validated its report) and `delivery` (one to sixteen references, such as the PR, merge commit and issue) permits retirement. A wrong provenance, repository, objective list, order or released paths refuses the whole decision `retirement_decision_mismatch`. Under the objective locks, taken in path order, Pod detects again, and each objective succeeds or refuses alone and is reported separately: one detection refuses keeps its own condition, and a malformed objective part or a fact that differs refuses `retirement_decision_mismatch` for that objective and writes nothing. Each objective it covers appends one `historical_retirement` entry to its record (time, decision digest, instruction, reason, repository, the objectives of the decision, owner, Runs, released paths and their R113 `locations`, preserved records, unfinished obligations, delivery references and a native evidence summary); that is its phase `retired`.
